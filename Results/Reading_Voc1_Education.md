## 📖 Bài đọc: The Impact of Higher Education Funding and Global Competition

# Reading: The Impact of Higher Education Funding and Global Competition

**Paragraph 1**
Education cuts have proven to be counterproductive to achieving our nation's long-term goals. The latest international league table shows that domestic universities and higher education institutes have performed comparatively poorly. While a few smaller departments managed to make progress in the right direction, several prestigious universities registered a sharp fall in the global ranking. In fact, third-level colleges were placed in a lower position than last year, and some fared worse than expected. Even when an institution recorded a modest rise of two spots, it was hardly anything to write home about.

**Paragraph 2**
Faced with these disappointing results, the university president was quick to blame recent cutbacks for the slippage. She did not hesitate to criticise the government's funding policy, arguing that taking funding out of third-level education will severely undermine the university's reputation. A well-educated workforce is vital in the long term, and persistent spending cuts will inevitably damage economic prospects for meaningful economic recovery in our debt-ridden country.

**Paragraph 3**
Academics highlight the futility of any attempt to compare domestic universities with their wealthy international counterparts. Top overseas universities charge each undergraduate student substantial fees per annum, and domestic institutions cannot withstand such excessive costs unless authorities reintroduce university fees. If funding continues to decline, our colleges will inevitably slide down the rankings, leaving policymakers with very few viable options.

**Paragraph 4**
To gain a foothold in the global education market, universities must attract foreign students from abroad. Tapping into the global education market has already proved to be highly lucrative for neighboring nations, and securing this source of revenue is crucial for institutional survival. However, relying on overseas fees is a double-edged sword: without a stellar international reputation, colleges cannot attract fee-paying students, and without their tuition fees, colleges lack the funding needed to improve. This creates a vicious circle. Ironically, competing on the global stage remains a central pillar of the current policy agenda, with ministers seeking to double the target number of international arrivals.

**Paragraph 5**
From a financial perspective, the underlying economics of recruiting foreign students appear completely sound. Currently, the average overseas learner spends tens of thousands of dollars on tuition fees and living expenses, delivering a massive boost and much-needed capital to the national economy. In the long run, these highly skilled graduates depart with an overwhelmingly positive impression of our country, opening up lucrative trading opportunities and long-term investment opportunities between our nations.

**Paragraph 6**
Nonetheless, it is neither sensible nor fair to divert funding away from domestic education in order to finance other budgets. These short-sighted spending cuts have provoked understandable anger among parents, students' groups, and teachers' associations. Student unions have threatened to organise a huge protest march at the slightest hint of support for tuition fees within government circles. The union president warned that demonstrations would bring public transport to a standstill and cause widespread disruption to local businesses and services. If the minister dares to approve this regressive proposal, students will not take it lying down. In an increasingly classist society, higher education must provide equal opportunity for every citizen to attend a degree course, rather than becoming the exclusive preserve of a wealthy elite from well-off backgrounds. Young people will not allow themselves to be burdened with huge debts, nor will the union stand idly by while educational fairness in their native country is systematically dismantled.
